Research

Learning the language of biology through computation.

My goal is to develop machine learning methods that help us reason about complex biological systems and design better therapeutics.

01

Current direction

I am preparing to begin my PhD in Computer Science & Engineering at the University of Washington, where I plan to work at the intersection of biology and computation.

I am especially interested in generative approaches—including diffusion and flow matching—for protein engineering and therapeutic design. The central question is both scientific and practical: how can models learn useful representations of biological constraints, and how can we turn those models into reliable tools for discovery?

02

Prior research

Informatics Skunkworks · UW–Madison

As an undergraduate researcher, I led and contributed to projects with the UW School of Medicine Department of Radiology. Our work explored machine learning on CT radiomics and clinical data for pancreatic cysts, renal angiomyolipomas, and renal cell carcinoma.

These projects taught me to work carefully with small, imbalanced clinical datasets; establish honest baselines; connect model behavior to domain knowledge; and recognize when the available data cannot support a confident conclusion.
